## Two Balances

Every Octra account has a public balance and an encrypted balance. The encrypted balance is a PVAC ciphertext: the network adds and subtracts ciphertexts (`ct_add` and `ct_sub`) without reading them, and only the key holder can decrypt. In the 0xio wallets every encryption, decryption and proof runs on your device. See [Public vs. Private Balances](/concepts/balances).

## Operations

| Operation | What happens |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Encrypt** | Public to private. The amount is encrypted, committed (Pedersen) and bound to the commitment with a zero proof. |
| **Decrypt** | Private to public, with a range proof that the remaining encrypted balance is not negative. |
| **Private send** | Sends an encrypted amount to a stealth output: an X25519 shared secret gives a stealth tag the recipient looks for, and the amount is encrypted to the recipient. Range proofs cover the amount and the new balance. |
| **Claim** | Moves a stealth output addressed to this wallet into its encrypted balance. The wallets claim only an output the node reads as unspent. |
| **Key switch** | Registers or refreshes the account's encryption key on chain. |

The 0xio wallets follow the PVAC implementation in Octra Labs' reference client [webcli](https://github.com/octra-labs/webcli) exactly, and refuse every private operation on a network that states no proof rules. See [Privacy Operations](/wallet/privacy-operations).

## What Is Hidden and What Is Not

- **Hidden:** the encrypted balance, the amount of a private send, and which account a private send is for (it lands as a stealth output that only the recipient can recognise).
- **Public:** the account that signs each transaction and pays its fee, and the amounts that cross between the public and encrypted balances on encrypt and decrypt.
- **Correlation:** encrypting an amount and decrypting the same amount soon after can be linked by amount and timing. Holding funds encrypted for longer and decrypting in different amounts makes that harder.

## Why FHE

| Approach | Examples | How it works | Limitation |
|----------|----------|--------------|------------|
| **Stealth addresses** | Umbra (Ethereum) | One-time addresses via ECDH | Amounts visible |
| **ZK shielded pools** | Railgun, Aztec | Prove facts about hidden data | Custom circuits per operation |
| **MPC** | Arcium, Secret Network | Computation shared among parties | Threshold trust assumptions |
| **FHE** | **Octra** | Computation directly on encrypted data | Performance overhead |

Octra's approach gives:

- **Encrypted arithmetic:** balances change on chain without being decrypted
- **No per-operation circuits:** the network works on ciphertexts directly
- **Any amount:** no fixed denominations, and an encrypted balance can be spent in part

The cost is proof work on the device. The desktop app runs it natively with every CPU core and can run it for the paired extension; see [0xio Desktop](/products/desktop#companion-mode-accelerating-the-extension).
